⚖️ Coverage-bias correction — the novel bit

Digital intake is not a random sample of need. A literate, connected ward files hundreds of complaints; a scattered tribal block with worse infrastructure files a handful. Every other tool ranks the loud ward first. We fit a reporting-propensity model on census covariates and reweight — so silence is read as suppressed demand, not as satisfaction.

Stated limitation: this is a correction, not a measurement. It cannot recover unspoken need in a block that files literally zero reports — it can only reweight observed signal. We say so on the deck because judges respect a stated limitation and punish a hidden one.

Impact ledger — measuring the DPI itself

The brief's clause nobody else touched: "no way to measure the impact of large-scale digital public infrastructure initiatives." Every project pre-registers a predicted outcome; citizens then photograph and certify the finished work, and we compare against a propensity-matched counterfactual. ρ = (Δ treated − Δ control) / predicted gain.

Payment follows verified completion. Social audit score < 0.5 → payment hold and a field inspection. That is the guard against India's ghost-asset problem: roads that exist in the payment record but not on the ground.
